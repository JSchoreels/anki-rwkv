# Per-user adaptation (SM143): result

## Read-out in plain language

- No variant passes. No adaptation of the longer Small Instant-Only model (SM113) improved LogLoss with a CI below 0 and at least 0.05%, so the pilot is a no go and the full 723-user run is not needed.
- Every change against the same model unadapted stays within about plus or minus 0.03% LogLoss and plus or minus 0.0002 AUC, for the longer Small Instant-Only model (SM113) and for the six-layer Tiny Instant-Only model (SM130). The largest are the full fine-tune (+0.03% LogLoss, 50 users) and the instant head (+0.02%) for the longer Small Instant-Only model (SM113).
- Adaptation hurts users with little history: for users with under 1,000 fit reviews, the retrained instant head costs +2.39% LogLoss (longer Small Instant-Only model (SM113)) and +1.02% (six-layer Tiny Instant-Only model (SM130)). For users with 20,000 or more fit reviews it gives only -0.01% to -0.03% for the longer Small Instant-Only model (SM113) (-0.08% at best for the six-layer Tiny Instant-Only model (SM130)).
- The models already adapt through their state: reading the user's history does what a per-user fit would do.
- Against FSRS-7 fitted per user on the same early reviews, the unadapted longer Small Instant-Only model (SM113) is -13.64% LogLoss [-16.67, -11.19] and +0.0905 within-user AUC (0.8278 vs 0.7372). The six-layer Tiny Instant-Only model (SM130) is -13.13% and +0.0877.
- The unadapted longer Small Instant-Only model (SM113) matches the show-time instant-only big model (SM99) on these 200 users: -0.01% LogLoss [-0.05, +0.03], AUC difference +0.0000, so the CI spans 0.
- Cost per user (all 5 folds): 0.30 to 0.72 s for the Platt control, the user vector and the instant head, 58 s mean (81 s at the 90th percentile) for the full fine-tune; a full-history forward takes 0.48 s (longer Small Instant-Only model (SM113)) and 0.20 s (six-layer Tiny Instant-Only model (SM130)).
