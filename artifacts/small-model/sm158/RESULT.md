# Output-aware T24 on the 732 set (SM158): result

Verdict: **no go.** Plain T24 stays the format for every size. Full tables: `sm158-result.md` and `sm158-result.json` (generated by `sm158_report.py report`, exit 0, decided).

## The question

At unchanged stored bytes, does T24 with output-aware truncation (receptance plus removal key weighting, label T24RK) cost less than plain T24 against int8 on the 732 set, by at least 0.05% on a head of each Big model, without making any head of any model significantly worse, and does it meet the 11-23 budgets? This is a confirmatory test of a hypothesis from the 8-user pointer output-aware low-rank truncation (SM157), whose own verdict (no go) stands.

## The rules (design.md, fixed before any weighted row existed)

Every figure is a review-weighted LogLoss difference in %, with a 95% paired user bootstrap on the 722 rule users of the 732 set (Big has 718 users, because the 5 largest users never fit the GPU). The budgets are on the CI upper bound per head: Big against its own int8 at most +0.15%; Small total against the matching Big model at int8 at most +0.5%; six-layer Tiny at most +1.0%. Go needs all of: (a) on each Big model at least one head where T24RK against T24 (D) has its CI upper bound below 0 and D at most -0.05%; (b) no head of any of the six models with the CI lower bound of D above 0; (c) every T24RK row finite and at least 715 users per model.

## Verdict

- (a) fails on both Big models: Big two-head (SM91s2) has its best head on instant with D -0.008% [-0.011%, -0.006%], and Big Instant-Only (SM99) instant has D -0.012% [-0.015%, -0.009%]. Both are significant improvements but far smaller than the required 0.05%.
- (b) holds: no head of any model has a CI lower bound of D above 0 for T24RK.
- (c) holds: all rows finite, 718 users for Big and 722 or 723 for Small and Tiny.
- Format per size: Tiny T24, Small T24, Big T24. Plain T24 meets every budget on every model and head (T24RK would too, but the gain does not justify the extra codec cost).

## Speed and codec cost

On the GPU the weighted labels run the torch step loop, not the fused kernel. T24RK review evaluations per second against plain T24: 0.519x for Big two-head (SM91s2), 0.519x for Big Instant-Only (SM99), 0.533x for Small two-head (SM118), 0.541x for Small Instant-Only (SM113), 0.488x for six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130), 0.491x for six-layer Tiny Instant-Only (SM130); Q2RK against plain Q2 is 0.487x to 0.540x. The CPU microbenchmark of the codec per write (one thread, rank 2, 4 heads): K = 16 weighted 22.6 us against plain 20.4 us (+11%); K = 32 weighted 54.0 us against 36.5 us (+48%). The weighting matrix M adds 40,960 bytes (Big), 6,144 (Small) and 4,096 (six-layer Tiny) to the model file at float16, full storage.

## GPU time

495 GPU minutes in total over 11 tickets (`gpu/tickets.jsonl`): smoke 97; Tiny 79 + 7; Small 105 + 1 + 1; Big 179 + 5 + 15 + 6.

## Warnings only: buckets by answers of the card before the review (T24RK against T24)

The 128+ bucket is worse with T24RK for 8 of 9 model heads (+0.013% to +0.085%; only the six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130) curve is -0.005%), while the 5-8 and 9-15 buckets improve by up to -0.058%. Long-card checks were not part of this test (design.md, rule 6). Full bucket table in `sm158-result.md`.

## Reading

Output-aware truncation gives a real but tiny gain (most heads -0.008% to -0.027%, significant on 8 of 9 heads), about a quarter or less of the 0.05% bar, at about half the GPU speed, with a codec cost of +11% to +48% per write and a possible loss on cards with 128+ answers. The 8-user pointer's gain on Big instant did not grow on the larger set. T24 stays.
