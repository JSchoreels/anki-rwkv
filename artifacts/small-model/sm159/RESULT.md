# Energy-triggered rank growth (SM159): result

**Verdict: NO GO** by the pre-registered rule in `design.md`. Both comparisons fail: the int8 factors against the shipped fixed schedule (T24, rank 2, then 4 from 16 answers) and the int6 factors against the same schedule at int6 (F6). The sixteen-answer age trigger of T24 stays. This is a CPU pointer on 8 probe users (the users of the low-rank structure probe (SM147)), so it is a pointer and not a gate. Aggregates only.

## What was run

- Codec: rank growth triggered by the discarded energy share (cap 2 at the start, grows by one up to 4 when the share is above tau), label suffix `g4e<code>`. Scored on the 8 probe users with show-time inputs, leak-free, CPU, on six models: Big two-head (SM91s2), Big Instant-Only (SM99), Small two-head (SM118), Small Instant-Only (SM113), six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130) and six-layer Tiny Instant-Only (SM130).
- Cost is the % change of the review-weighted benchmark LogLoss against int8 rows (REF), with a paired user bootstrap 95% CI. Bytes cover all answered cards and notes of the 8 users, in the fork's compact layout.
- Proof: 144 rows compared, OK. The int8 reference, rank 2 (Q2) and T24 rows equal the rows of the output-aware truncation pointer (SM157), and the rank read-out equals the answer-count formula.

## Run history

- The first run of the chain failed at 11:11 on 11-24. The launcher read the label lists generated by Python with Windows CRLF line endings, so every label ended in `\r` and the scorer stopped with `unknown variant w8+s8hx8`. The main session fixed it in 283c5422 (`tr -d '\r'`) and relaunched at 11:13. The calibration was reused (it had finished at 11:11). The run ended at 11:55 with `SM159 RUN DONE`. The first-line errors in `cpu/probe-{a,b,c}.out` come from the failed first attempt.

## Matched thresholds

- The training-matched thresholds (calibrated on 20 training users, equal bytes to T24 there) were: Big two-head (SM91s2) 0.1795, Big Instant-Only (SM99) 0.1818, Small two-head (SM118) 0.1708, Small Instant-Only (SM113) 0.1746, six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130) 0.1487, six-layer Tiny Instant-Only (SM130) 0.1530.
- On the probe users those labels (EM, EM6) gave about 6% to 7% fewer bytes than T24 (Big two-head (SM91s2) -6.7%, Small two-head (SM118) -7.1%, six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130) -6.9%), because the probe users include long-card users, while on the training users T24 grows few entities (it is only 0.7% to 0.8% above rank 2 there). They are therefore not a matched comparison on the probe users.
- The pre-registered fallback fixed this: probe-matched labels EMP (int8 factors) and EMP6 (int6 factors) per model, from the bytes of the scored energy labels only. **The verdict table below, `sm159-result.json` (`match`) and `sm159-verdict.json` use EMP against T24 and EMP6 against F6 for every model.** Their bytes are within 0.4% of T24 (Big two-head (SM91s2) -0.21%, Big Instant-Only (SM99) -0.15%, Small two-head (SM118) -0.30%, Small Instant-Only (SM113) -0.29%, six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130) -0.38%, six-layer Tiny Instant-Only (SM130) -0.07%); EMP6 is within 0.33% of F6.

## Rule

- T24 comparison: two cells are informative (T24 costs more than 0.05%, so there is something to reduce): the instant heads of Big two-head (SM91s2) and six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130). In both, the matched energy label costs more than T24 (cost reduction -0.66% and -2.71% of T24's cost). Four cells are worse than T24: the instant heads of Big two-head (SM91s2), Small Instant-Only (SM113), six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130) and six-layer Tiny Instant-Only (SM130). Pass: no.
- F6 comparison: the same two informative cells, both worse (-0.31% and -1.71%); five worse cells (adding Big Instant-Only (SM99)). Pass: no.
- Verdict file: `{"decided": true, "pass_T24": false, "pass_F6": false, "go": false, "inconclusive": false}`.

## Rank distribution

- T24 puts 10.6% of answered cards and 15.3% of answered notes at rank 4 (exactly the entities with 16 or more answers), the rest at rank 2 (rank 1 for a single answer).
- The matched energy labels (EMP) spread the ranks instead. Big two-head (SM91s2): 17.3% of cards and 33.0% of notes end at rank 2, 53.9% and 51.0% at rank 3, 17.0% and 4.4% at rank 4. Small two-head (SM118): 17.0% and 48.9% at rank 2, 68.4% and 38.9% at rank 3, 2.8% and 0.6% at rank 4. Six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130): 27.3% and 53.7% at rank 2, 59.0% and 34.3% at rank 3, 1.8% and 0.5% at rank 4.
- Among entities with 16 or more answers, the matched label for Big two-head (SM91s2) gives rank 4 to 54.6% of cards and 11.5% of notes (rank 3: 45.3% and 82.5%); for Small two-head (SM118) rank 4 to 4.9% and 2.3% (rank 3: 93.5% and 82.8%); for six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130) 5.0% and 1.5% (rank 3: 84.9% and 73.8%). The energy rule spends rank on young entities with an early burst of energy and gives long-lived ones mostly rank 3, which is where it loses against T24 on the instant heads.

## At T24's cost, fewer bytes

The scored int8 energy label with the fewest bytes whose point cost is at most T24's on every head of the model: none for Small Instant-Only (SM113), six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130) and six-layer Tiny Instant-Only (SM130); E500 for Big two-head (SM91s2) and Big Instant-Only (SM99), which has more bytes than T24 (+9.1% and +9.3%), so no saving; E1500 for Small two-head (SM118), with -6.4% bytes vs T24 and point costs within 0.01% of T24's (CIs include 0). The Small two-head (SM118) read-out is below the 0.05% minimum effect and is not a result on 8 users.

## What it means

At matched bytes, the energy trigger is no better than the age trigger at 16 answers, and it is worse on the instant heads of the Tiny models and of Big two-head (SM91s2) (paired cost differences up to +0.05%, CIs above 0 for the instant heads of Big two-head (SM91s2) and six-layer Tiny two-head (SM130), and at the edge for six-layer Tiny Instant-Only (SM130)). On the curve heads and on the Small models everything is inside the noise. T24's fixed schedule stays. No GPU follow-up is proposed.
